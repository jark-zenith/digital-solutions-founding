# Founding Roadmap — First 30, 60 and 90 Days
**Status:** Proposed execution plan · Dates begin after the founding kickoff

## Days 1–30 — Establish the foundation
**Objectives:** Evaluate interested candidates and agree on the founding direction.

- Monitor the number of people expressing interest before setting a starting team size.
- Prepare and share a Google Sheet for structured founder information when ready.
- Review skills, practical evidence, availability and motivation consistently after responses are received.
- Hold the first founding meeting and record decisions.
- Agree the company-name selection process and shortlist.
- Review the business concept and proposed customer segments.
- Identify provisional role owners and immediate skill gaps.
- Discuss governance, contributions, decision rights and legal pathway.
- Select a small number of services to validate first.

**Evidence of progress:** Candidate interest overview, completed information sheet, evaluation record, meeting minutes, approved concept direction, name shortlist and provisional work plan.

## Days 31–60 — Prepare to operate
**Objectives:** Establish a credible delivery and customer-facing foundation.

- Refine service packages, scope templates and pricing assumptions.
- Build a simple portfolio with clearly labelled demonstrations.
- Prepare proposal, quotation, invoice and project handover templates.
- Establish project management, communication and file-access rules.
- Finalize founders' agreement and company formation documents with professional advice.
- Define financial controls, expense approvals and bookkeeping process.
- Conduct customer discovery interviews with target businesses.
- Validate the website, domain and public contact process.

**Evidence of progress:** Service catalogue, portfolio, customer feedback, documented workflows and reviewed governance documents.

## Days 61–90 — Validate and launch carefully
**Objectives:** Secure early customer work and learn from real delivery.

- Build a qualified prospect list and conduct direct outreach.
- Issue proposals for clearly scoped work.
- Accept projects only where the team has capacity and delivery competence.
- Deliver initial work with testing, customer acceptance and support handover.
- Record revenue, direct costs, time spent and project margin.
- Review customer feedback and identify repeatable needs.
- Select product experiments based on evidence rather than assumptions.
- Review team participation and update provisional responsibilities.

**Evidence of progress:** Qualified leads, proposals, paid projects where secured, delivery records, financial review and prioritized product hypotheses.

## Operating cadence
- Weekly team check-in and blocker review.
- Written owner and due date for each task.
- Customer scope and price approved before work starts.
- Monthly financial and progress review.
- Decisions and changes recorded centrally.

## Risks and adjustment
The roadmap is a target, not a guarantee of revenue or registration by a fixed date. Adjust for team capacity, customer demand, legal requirements and available resources. Do not take on work the team cannot responsibly deliver.
