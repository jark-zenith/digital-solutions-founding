# Business Model & Financial Plan
**Status:** Initial planning draft · Figures and prices require market validation

## 1. Revenue model
The proposed company combines:
- Project fees for websites, web applications, QR menus and digital business cards.
- Packages for branding, promotional media and digital marketing.
- Setup and implementation fees for AI automation and integrations.
- Recurring maintenance, hosting management and support agreements.
- Future subscriptions, licences or usage fees from proprietary products.

Prices should be quoted per defined scope after understanding requirements, delivery effort, third-party costs, support obligations and customer budget. Do not promise fixed prices before scope is clear.

## 2. Customer and project controls
For each project, prepare a written scope, deliverables, exclusions, milestones, timeline, payment schedule, change-control process, acceptance criteria, support terms and IP provisions. Where commercially appropriate, request a deposit before starting work. Track direct costs against each project.

## 3. Cash-flow sequence
Customer receipts are not automatically profit. Record receipts and deduct or reserve for direct delivery costs, refunds, payment charges, taxes and other business obligations as advised by the accountant. Keep a working-capital buffer so the company can complete accepted work.

## 4. Proposed profit allocation
The founder's current proposal is:
- **40% of net profit:** target business reinvestment allocation.
- **Remaining 60% of net profit:** allocation to be discussed and agreed by the founding team.

This is a proposal, not an adopted policy or guaranteed payout. The definition of net profit, timing of allocation, cash reserve, tax treatment, approval process and treatment of losses must be agreed with professional advice. Do not allocate a percentage of gross receipts as though it were profit.

## 5. Potential reinvestment priorities
Subject to approved budgets and actual need:
1. Domain registration and website hosting.
2. Business registration and professional/legal/accounting costs.
3. Essential software, development tools and security.
4. Marketing, sales and portfolio development.
5. Equipment or infrastructure that has a clear return.
6. Product research and validated prototypes.

## 6. Financial controls
- Use a dedicated business account when legally and practically available.
- Maintain a transaction ledger and retain invoices/receipts.
- Separate personal and business money.
- Require documented approval for expenditure.
- Use two-person review for material payments where feasible.
- Reconcile records monthly and produce a simple income/expense/cash report.
- Agree who may quote, invoice, collect and approve refunds.
- Seek Kenyan tax and accounting advice before trading at scale.

## 7. Compensation
Payment for project work, reimbursement, salaries, contractor fees, retained earnings and shareholder dividends are separate categories. Agree project compensation before delivery begins. Dividends, if any, are subject to law, company solvency, formal approvals and shareholder rights.

## 8. Planning assumptions and risks
The venture starts with limited capital and no guaranteed salary. Risks include slow customer acquisition, underpricing, scope creep, delayed payments, uneven contributions, technology costs, data/security incidents and disagreements over ownership. Mitigate through small scopes, deposits, written approvals, delivery reviews and transparent records.

## 9. First financial milestones
- Establish a baseline cost list.
- Price three initial service packages using real delivery estimates.
- Complete the first paid project with documented costs and margin.
- Build a cash reserve before discretionary distributions.
- Review the 40/60 proposal after actual trading data is available.
